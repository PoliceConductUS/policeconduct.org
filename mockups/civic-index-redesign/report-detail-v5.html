Public report

First Amendment retaliation arrest

A first-party account of an arrest during a police encounter in Irving, Texas, linked to public records and body-camera video.

Incident date
Dec 4, 2023
Location
5910 N MacArthur Blvd, Irving, TX 75039
Agency
Irving Police Department
Submitted by
Person directly involved
Charge outcome
Dismissed after suppression

What happened

The submitter reported that Officer James Markham surveilled him in a public place for more than 15 minutes without identifying a crime or safety emergency. When the submitter asked about the surveillance, Markham repeatedly said the submitter was free to go. The submitter replied that if he was free to go, he was free to stay.

After the submitter criticized the surveillance and said he did not want to be subjected to profiling, additional Irving officers arrived. The submitter reported that no field sobriety tests were conducted and that Markham arrested him for public intoxication after the protected criticism.

The submitter reported that, from the moment officers initiated physical contact, he initiated no movement of his own. He reported that he offered no physical resistance, made no verbal protest, and produced no audible noise during the application of pain-compliance holds.

At Irving City Jail, the submitter reported being placed in a cold cell without shoes, a mattress, or a blanket. He reported that requests to contact a lawyer were not honored and that jail staff threatened suicide restraints to compel booking answers after he invoked his right to remain silent.

Records and evidence

Public body-worn camera videos are linked to this report. The related federal civil-rights case is Lotts v. City of Irving et al, No. 3:25-CV-03329-S-BN (N.D. Tex.). Those allegations remain pending unless and until resolved by the court.

People involved

The submitter identified James Markham (badge 1379) as the officer who surveilled and arrested him, and Andrew Hammett (badge 1263) and Paul Lewis (badge 1370) as officers involved in the encounter. All three hold Peace Officer licenses at Irving Police Department. Irving City Jail staff were identified as involved in booking and jail conditions.

Why this report was shared

Preserve what happened, help others see a pattern, support accountability or review, and add context to officer and agency records.

This report is a processed version of a submitted first-party report. It reflects the submitter's account and linked public records. Publication is not a court finding.

How reports work

Facts, not feelings

  • Reports describe observations — what was said and done — not star ratings or opinions.
  • Strong reports are chronological and concrete, name the people involved, and link supporting records.
  • Every report is reviewed and organized before publication and stays connected to the agency and personnel records it names.
