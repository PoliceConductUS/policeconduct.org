Two load paths exist for this corpus and the change is correct under both. Loader-written provenance columns on the row take precedence; in their absence the citation comes from a ledger derived from the source artifact and the identity map recorded when it was loaded. **The transitional path is the one assumed here**, because it is what the deployed build reads.

`field-suppression.v3.json` holds the policy as data, so a change to what the site withholds is reviewable as a diff by someone who does not read TypeScript. Its rules divide into accuracy rules, which withhold a value because it is wrong about a named person even though its lineage is perfect, and provenance rules, which withhold a value because we cannot show where it came from. Accuracy rules are enforced unconditionally. Provenance rules follow `PROVENANCE_ENFORCEMENT`, which defaults to reporting rather than withholding until ledger coverage has been measured against the database the build reads — enforcing on an unmeasured ledger could withhold names on pages whose agreement with their source was measured at 100%, which is a takedown by other means.

Classification is not configurable. A dataset with no attested retrieval date cannot be called traceable however much else is known about it, and the citation states that the retrieval date is missing instead of implying one. The page's subject name is deliberately outside the gate: it is already published in the URL, the canonical link and the sitemap, so withholding it removes the page rather than the claim.
